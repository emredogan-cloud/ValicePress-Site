## Phase 2 — the registry is tested against the world  ✔

The registry already existed — `scripts/catalog/valice-catalog.mjs` is the one file that says what the store sells, and the loader applies it to the database. What was missing was anything that **proved the registry agrees with reality**. Added `scripts/catalog/catalog-identity.test.ts` (13 tests): slugs, ASINs and ISBNs are unique across the whole catalogue; a series number is used once; every Amazon URL is the one its ASIN produces; no ASIN belongs to two books; a KDP-Select ebook is never offered here (the existing guard, untouched); and **every ASIN has a row of evidence read from the live Amazon `/dp/` page — title, format and ISBN — that agrees with the registry** (`data/catalog/amazon-verification.json`).

**What it found, on the first run**
- two of the 48 existing ASINs were wrong, both World Games: the Kindle ASIN `B0HG44FH1B` does not exist on Amazon, and the "hardcover" `B0HG41F21F` is a different format of the older 56-game edition (`AUDIT/data/amazon-asins-catalog-BEFORE-2026-10-07.json`);
- "Valice Classics #15" was used by two books (`puzzles-old-and-new` carried an unverified volume number) — removed rather than invented.

**The verifier** (`scripts/catalog/verify-amazon-asins.mjs`) is resumable and now fetches with `curl`. Node's `fetch` gets Amazon's ~2 KB robot-check stub for pages that `curl`, with identical headers, reads in full: three `fetch` passes left 16 ASINs "throttled"; one `curl` pass read all 16.

**Order is data, deterministic.** `PINNED_BOOK_SLUGS` + `byPinnedRank` apply last and never add a book; the remainder is `published_at desc nulls last, slug asc` (`src/lib/shelf-order.ts`, tested). Before: ties fell back to whatever order Postgres returned.

## Phase 4 — a preview for every book, from the book's own words  ✔

**Every one of the 37 books now has the same four panels: front cover, back cover, two passages** — or, where the book has no printed edition and therefore no back cover, front, two passages and one of the book's own interior pages. Nothing is borrowed from another book and nothing is invented: a back cover is cropped from the book's paperback wrap (27 books), and when there is no wrap there is no back cover.

**The passages are the book's.** `scripts/previews/quote-picks.json` says *where* each passage is (file, paragraph, the words it starts and stops at) and nothing about what it says; `select-quotes.py` cuts the words out of the manuscript or the typeset PDF; `verify-quotes.mjs` proves each cut is **contiguous, in order, with nothing dropped**, against the manuscript (paragraph-exact) **and** against the printed interior (flat) — 74 passages, 0 failures — and writes the proof (file, SHA-256, date, method) next to the quote. A negative control confirmed it rejects a changed word, a reordered clause and a dropped sentence. `book-media.test.ts` (199 tests) refuses a passage without its proof, a card that is not the file that was ingested, and two books sharing a quotation.

**The typography is ours.** `quote-card.py` sets every glyph from font files (EB Garamond, Cinzel); an image generator never spells the book's words. Backgrounds: the two Weather Permitting cards use a generated night-chalet plate (`scripts/previews/plates/`, disclosed in the provenance record); every other card uses a soft-focus crop of **that book's own cover** — chosen so no lettering of the cover survives (earlier attempts leaked cover titles into the blur; they were caught on the contact sheets and the crops moved). Parchment/ink themes follow the cover: games and workbooks on paper, mythologies on dark.

**Editorial notes are labelled.** Where a passage is from this edition's own notes rather than the author's text, the card says so ("Editor's note · …"); a passage from an author is never set under the editor's name or the reverse.

### Notes
- The ChatGPT image path (the brief's suggestion for backgrounds) was tried and abandoned for all but one plate: its downloads open a native save dialog that freezes the automated tab. The covers' own art gives each book a distinct, truthful atmosphere with no generative step.
- `pdf_blocks.py` reads a typeset PDF as paragraphs (PyMuPDF) and strips glyphs the PDF could not map; it caught a NUL-character artefact in one passage before it reached a card.

## Phase 7 — an authors directory of 31 people, each with sources  ✔

`/authors` lists **31 people, once each**: 28 researched authors (the 25 asked for — 10 historical, 5 of games and puzzles, 10 of sapphic and lesbian romance — plus three more public-domain authors Valice publishes, so every one of them has a full page) and the three living authors Valice publishes, whose pages are built only from the catalogue's approved biographies. Search (name, a book, a place; accent-insensitive), filters with counts that are counted not typed, sorts by surname / most books / birth year, a card for each, and a photograph-credits list under the grid. Every card opens `/authors/<slug>`: credited portrait, life dates and places, biography, major works, a timeline, why they matter, their Valice titles (if any), "nearby on the shelf", related authors, sources. Structured data names the dates and links Wikipedia, Wikidata and the Library of Congress; all 31 are in the sitemap.

**The rule that governs the page: two kinds of person, never blurred.** An author **on the Valice list** has a published title in the catalogue (an edition of their public-domain work, or an original) and the card and page say so. Everyone else is a **reference author**, and their page says in plain words that Valice Press has no connection with them, their publishers or their estate. No follower counts, no "partner", no endorsement; "nearby on the shelf" says the titles are *not by* the author and not a recommendation on their behalf. The published/reference status comes from the catalogue, not from the dataset, so it cannot be wrong by editing a JSON file; a test fails if a reference author is marked published or the reverse. (The identity mark's caption used to be hard-wired to "Valice Press author", which would have been false for four of these; it is gone.)

**Where the facts come from, and how that is enforced.** One JSON file per author (`src/content/authors/`), each listing its sources, written in our own words from them. `scripts/authors/verify-sources.mjs` fetches every source and fails unless the source answers and names the person; **the birth and death years agree between Wikidata and the Library of Congress authority record** (two independent compilations, the latter by librarians from the books); every year in the works and timeline appears in the sources; and each key claim — an award, an epithet, a title — is in the sources word for word. Result recorded in `data/authors/verification.json` (28/28), and a unit test fails if an author's sources change without a re-run. A report generated from the data is `AUDIT/AUTHOR-DIRECTORY-SOURCES.md`. **What the verifier caught:** 14 of the 28 Wikidata ids I had typed from memory were wrong (a wrong id points at someone else), and so was one Library of Congress id; both are now looked up, never typed (`sync-wikidata.mjs`, `resolve-loc.mjs`). It also found two real disagreements, recorded on the pages: the LoC gives H. J. R. Murray's birth as 1869 against 1868 in Wikipedia, Wikidata and the Oxford Companion to Chess; and Henry Lee's birth is 1826 or 1827 in the sources.

**Photographs: 22 of 28, every one from Wikimedia Commons, none guessed.** `fetch-portraits.mjs` refuses anything outside public domain / CC0 / CC BY / CC BY-SA, anything with a Commons restriction flag, and any credit line the Commons record (including the file's own wikitext) does not support — that last check is why Ian Livingstone's credit names the Flickr photographer "FrankBoyd" rather than the graphics-lab banner in the structured field. Files are decoded and re-encoded by us at 3:4, never enlarged, with the credit, licence, source SHA-1 and published SHA-256 recorded; the licences are 12 public domain, 3 CC BY-SA 4.0, 2 CC BY-SA 3.0, 2 CC BY 2.0, 2 CC0, 1 CC BY-SA 2.0. A portrait that is not a photograph says what it is: Ovid's is "a bust assumed to be Ovid" (Uffizi); T. H. Thomas's is Goscombe John's 1903 bust; Mary Shelley's is Rothwell's 1840 painting. Six people have the initials mark and a line under it ("not a likeness") saying why.

## Phase 9 — a cart that belongs to one visitor, a plus that adds, arrows that scroll  ✔

**The worst defect was not on the list.** `src/lib/cart.ts` kept one module-level `EMPTY_CART = { items: [] }` and returned that very object whenever the cart cookie was missing or unreadable — and `addToCart` pushed into whatever `readCart()` gave it. So the first add from a browser with no cart cookie wrote that book into the process-wide "empty" cart, and from then on **every cookie-less visitor on that server instance opened `/cart` to a stranger's book**; pressing "+" on that title answered "already in your cart" *without writing a cookie* (the button says yes, the badge stays 0, the cart is empty in your browser). It was found by noticing a fixture change under me — the eighth card of the cart's shelf swapped between two of my own runs with no data change — and following it with `curl`: a request with **no cookies** returned "1 book ready", an explicit empty cookie returned "Your cart is empty". No test could have found it (the old tests compared with `toEqual`, which is structural). Reproduced on the local server, then fixed at the root: every parse returns a new object and every cart operation (`addItem`, `removeItem`, `pruneItems`) is pure and copies. **On production I could only look** — six cookie-less GETs of `valicepress.com/cart` all showed an empty cart; pressing "+" there would pollute a live instance, so I did not. The code path is identical, and Vercel reuses function instances, so this is a plausible cause of the reported "the plus doesn't add the adjacent book" — a hypothesis to check by repeating your steps after deploy, not something I have confirmed.

**What else the audit found, and what changed** (all inside the existing cookie + Server Action cart — no second cart was built; the only new client piece, `cart-store.ts`, *reads* the cart, it does not hold one):

## Phase 10 — one admin area, with real data, working email, and a gate that is tested  ✔

**What was there.** Five admin pages that shared nothing — no layout, five hand-rolled gates (two copies of the error mapper), the public site's header and footer, and a way between them only through three links on the dashboard. The dashboard wore a **create-book form and per-book edit / publish / hard-delete actions that wrote catalogue rows straight into the database** — bypassing the catalogue tests and the KDP-Select rule, overwritten by the next catalogue load, and the exact thing `CLAUDE.md` forbids ("never edit catalog rows in the database"). A failed query came back as zeros (`safeQuery` → "0 orders · 0 users"), so an outage read like a quiet day. The email page was **read-only** and, worse, **an unsubscribe never reached it**: the unsubscribe route talked only to Resend, so the "Suppressed" and "Mailable" counts the whole page is built around could not move. And `requireAdmin` — the one door every admin page, query, action and API route passes through — **had no test at all**.

**What there is now.** One shell (`/admin`, six tabs: Overview · Books · Email · Free books · Reader support · Site data), one gate, and the legacy pages gone (`/admin/contacts`, `/admin/books/<slug>/edit` and the create/delete actions are removed; the old addresses redirect into the new ones).

- **The gate (`lib/auth.ts`, `lib/admin/*`).** The decision is a pure function, `evaluateAdminCandidate`, with every branch tested (35 tests, 4 mutations caught): empty allow-list lets nobody in, the PRIMARY address decides (an allow-listed *secondary* address elevates nobody), and — new — **the address must be verified by Clerk** (a missing verification record counts as unverified), asked AFTER "is it on the list?" so an unverified stranger learns nothing about the list. A refusal now says only that it is a refusal: the old pages printed `err.message`, which read "User a@b.com is not on ADMIN_EMAILS". The reader-support queries, which return a customer's entitlements by address, were gated only by their page; they now gate themselves like every other admin query. The four copies of `tokenAccepted` are one tested helper, and a route that *does* something (storage and fulfilment probes that write and delete objects, Sentry `?emit=1`, the email-check send) **refuses a `Sec-Fetch-Site: cross-site` request even with a valid admin cookie** — cookies ride a cross-site GET, so a page on any other site could previously have made an admin's browser run them. `robots.txt` now disallows `/admin` itself (it had `/admin/`, which does not cover the dashboard's own address).
- **Overview — real data only.** Every figure is read on its own and is one of three things: *read* (zero included), *unavailable* (no source, or the table is not on this database) or *error* (a source that failed). Where there is no source it says so in words: **"Sales data unavailable — no connected sales source"** for Amazon/KDP, and page views. Catalogue counts (published, sold-here, ebook/paperback/hardcover/large-print titles), the featured books in pin order (the draft one flagged), mailable subscribers and latest signups *with their evidence*, free-book queue by status, direct orders recorded by the payment webhook (said plainly to include test-mode orders, which the table cannot tell apart), funnel events, downloads, popup outcomes. The sandbox has no `analytics_events` table, and the dashboard shows exactly that — "Not available on this database yet" — instead of "0 events".
- **Books — read-only.** No form, no edit link; "Not sold here" instead of "$0.00".
- **Email — the part that was asked for.** Add, edit details, suppress, record an opt-in, mark not-a-marketing-contact, delete, search, filter (status / consent / source / audience), sort, page, **find duplicates**, **export (whole view, paged, CSV formulas defused)**, with the consent policy in pure rules rather than in the form: a new contact is *not subscribed*; the only way to a mailable one is to state, in words, how and when they agreed (stored as `admin: …`); **nobody who unsubscribed can be re-subscribed from here** (only they can, on a form); an opt-in cannot be quietly relabelled "not a marketing contact" (that is a suppression); deleting a suppressed contact needs an explicit "erase the suppression record too"; a change is conditional on the row still being unsuppressed, so a person unsubscribing while the page is open is not overwritten. Duplicates are detected by mailbox, not spelling (Gmail dots and `+tags`, `googlemail.com`; `+tags` at Outlook / iCloud / Proton) — as a *warning with an "add anyway"* and a *report*, never a silent merge. **Unsubscribes now reach the contact book** (an address the book has never seen gets a suppression row; a throw-away mailbox is still honoured).
- **Free books** moved under the shell with a `sending` filter, search and paging, and the stale "15-minute link" sentence replaced by what the code does (up to 20 MB as an attachment, anything larger as a link that works for 72 hours — read from the constants). **Reader support** moved in. **Site data** is new and shows only the three tables that record visitors.

**Four defects the new tests found in the new code** (each was fixed, and each is now pinned): (1) Drizzle wraps the Postgres error, so a missing table was reported as a generic failure — found only by running the queries against the real driver; (2) React 19 **clears an uncontrolled `<form action>` after every submission**, so a typo'd address or an alias warning wiped everything the administrator had typed — found by a component test ("add anyway" sent an empty address); (3) a React-controlled `<details open>` closed the Add form the moment it succeeded, taking the confirmation with it — found by the browser test; (4) the "Suppressed." confirmation vanished because the panel that showed it stops existing once the contact is suppressed — also the browser test; it now lives in a region that stays. A fifth, a build-time one: Next's route-type check rejects `validateEventPayload` exported from `src/app/api/events/route.ts`; the Turbopack build the site ships with does not enforce it, webpack does — noted for Phase 12. A sixth, found by a browser test that would not go quiet: every link in the admin shell was prefetched by Next, so opening any admin page also fetched the public homepage and every contact's page — **database reads on dynamic routes, for pages nobody had asked for** — and kept the network busy for 28–60 s. All admin links now say `prefetch={false}`.

**One defect in OLDER code, found because one test in 582 failed once.** The full browser run had a single red: Firefox, `page.goto("/cart")` rejected with `NS_BINDING_ABORTED`. It had passed in isolation, so "flake" was the easy verdict. The trace said otherwise: 48 ms after the navigation began, the page it was leaving logged *"Failed to fetch RSC payload for /books. Falling back to browser navigation."* The cause is in `catalog-shell.tsx`, untouched by this branch: the catalogue keeps its filters in the address bar and **wrote the address ~300 ms after mounting even when nothing had changed** — a `router.replace` to the page it was already on, on every visit to `/books` and `/ebooks`. On a warm router that is answered from cache and invisible; on a cold or slow one (a phone on a bad connection) it is a server round trip for nothing, and **when the visitor leaves while it is in flight, Next falls back to a browser navigation to the page they were leaving.** Reproduced on purpose by holding every RSC response for 1.5 s: in Firefox a hard navigation to `/cart` 450 ms after `/books` loaded was **aborted 8 of 8 times and the browser ended on `/books`**; clicking a header link 100 ms after load was swallowed 2 of 12 times (0 of 12 on `/authors`, which has no such write). One guard fixes it (write only if the query differs from what the address already holds — the ref that tracked exactly that was already there); it is its own commit, `33d6dbe`, so it can be read or reverted apart from the admin work. **Red before, green after:** 6 component tests (5 fail with the guard removed; the sixth, "a change is still written, once", rightly does not) and 4 browser tests × 3 projects (12 in all). On the old build the plain-visit tests failed in Chromium and on the phone ("the page re-requested itself") and the leave-straight-away test failed in Firefox with the original `NS_BINDING_ABORTED` (and passed in Chromium, where the fallback does not happen); on the fixed build all 12 pass. The failing run had not been a flake; it was the one run, of 582 tests, whose timing was fine enough to show a real behaviour.

**How the signed-in half is tested without a bypass in the code.** A test machine cannot sign in to Clerk, and the identity check must not contain a way around itself. So `npm run test:e2e:admin` (`scripts/e2e/admin-harness.mjs`) builds a **throw-away copy of the repository in /tmp** with the identity function replaced by exact-string patches (it fails loudly if a pattern is missing), runs it on **127.0.0.1 only**, against the sandbox database only, with every third-party key blanked, and runs `e2e/admin.pw.ts` against it. The shipped code has no flag, header or variable that opens the admin area. (`e2e/admin-security.pw.ts` runs against the normal build and checks the other half: a stranger gets a sign-in redirect or a refusal, no content, no CSV, no action, no API.)

## Phase 11 — one geometry for every book card  ✔

**What was there** (measured on `/books` and `/ebooks`, every page, Chromium, at the brief's widths): at 1440 px the cards in one grid came in **8 different heights (452–548 px)**, at 320 px 7 (318–400 px). Three causes, all in `catalog-book-card.tsx`: the `<article>` did not fill its grid cell (about half the cards — 25 of 48 measured instances — ended above the bottom of their row, so one panel was shorter than its neighbour); the title was clamped to two lines but not *given* two lines, so on a card with a one-line title the author sat 20.7 px higher than on its neighbour; and the format chips wrapped — between chips, and *inside* a chip ("Paperback · Hardcover · Large / Print" became a three-line pill) — so a card held one to three rows of them. On top of the covers sat a **wishlist heart that has no handler** (the schema calls wishlist "reserved for a future feature") and a **lock whose tooltip says "Locked — buy to unlock"**, which is not true of a book you can only buy on Amazon; together they hid the last letters of "WORLD GAMES", "WEATHER PERMITTING", "CODEX BESTIARIUM" and "HARPER HAYES" on the covers. The list view painted a *typographic stand-in* (a gradient and the first two words of the title) over books that have real covers, and had no link to the book's page, only a "Quick view" button. And the sort menu offered **"Top Rated" over a catalogue with no ratings**, and sorted a book with no price (`price_cents = 0` means *not sold here*) to the *top* of "Price: Low → High", as if it were the cheapest book in the shop.

**What there is now: one geometry** (`.catalog-card*` in `globals.css`; the card is its own container, so what it prints depends on the room it has, not on the viewport).
- **Every variable area is reserved, not measured.** The cover is a 2:3 frame; the title has the lines it may take (3 below 212 px of card content, 2 from there; a list row: 3, 2, or 1 by width) and ends in an ellipsis if it needs more; the author is one line; the page count is its own quiet line (text, no longer a third chip) with room for the gift box beside it; the chips have two rows reserved (one where two short chips always fit side by side). A card that needs less leaves the room empty at the foot of its own block and stays the same size. `article` now fills its cell.
- **Nothing is hidden to achieve it.** A title cut by its lines is whole in the link's name, in the native tooltip, in Quick View and on its page. A chip too long for a narrow card prints a **short form** of the same fact (`Badge.compact`: "eBook · PDF + EPUB" → "PDF + EPUB", "Paperback · Hardcover · Large Print" → "Paperback +2"); the full label is `sr-only` on every card, and printed in full from 212 px of card (a 1440 px desktop and up).
- **Covers are scaled, never stretched.** The frame is 2:3; the image fills it with `object-fit: cover`/`contain` per the existing `coverFit` rule (unchanged). 22 of the 36 covers are within 0.2 % of 2:3; 14 are 5:8 (316 × 506) and lose 3.2 % of their height at the top and at the bottom — the sliver the old rule already accepted ("reads fine"). Say if you would rather see those whole, on a thin matte.
- **List view is the same card, turned on its side**: the same 2:3 cover (the real one), the same reserved title / author / page line / chips, one row height at a given width, and the whole row is a link like a grid card (a plain click opens Quick View; ⌘-click or no JavaScript goes to the page). The separate "Quick view" button is gone.
- **The sort menu is honest.** Unpriced books sort *after* every priced one, in both directions; "Top Rated" and the price sorts are offered only when they would order something (a link that asks for one that is not on offer gets the default order instead of a dropdown with no matching option).

## Phase 12 — what a crawler, a screen reader, a keyboard and a slow phone each make of the site  ✔

Method: three instruments first, then fixes — `scripts/seo/audit.mjs` (crawls the sitemap and the unlisted public routes as Googlebot; 131 pages, 248 internal link targets, 59 social images), `axe-core` 4.14 over 28 pages at a desktop and a phone size, and `scripts/perf/page-weight.mjs` (every image, script and cache header a page costs, on a phone profile and a desktop one). All three are in the repo and re-runnable against production (`--base https://valicepress.com`) — Phase 15 will.

**The finding that mattered: `/books` and `/ebooks` sent an empty catalogue.** The shell called `useSearchParams()`, which makes Next render its whole Suspense subtree on the client only; the server's HTML held a grey placeholder and **no card, no cover and no link to a book** (0 of 36 in the HTML — measured with a crawler's user-agent). Googlebot runs JavaScript and gets there eventually; a link preview, a no-JS reader, Bing's first pass and a phone before hydration did not. It also bought the first cover its late start: **LCP 5.9 s on a throttled mid-range phone** (4× CPU, 1.6 Mbps, 150 ms). The shell now renders on the server in its default view (newest, page one, grid) and the address bar is read by a child that renders nothing (`AddressReader`, own `<Suspense>`), which applies `?page=2&view=list&sort=…` once the browser has it. **12 cards and 12 book links in the server's HTML on both routes; LCP 5.9 → 3.1 s; CLS 0.** (The cover is still the LCP element; the rest of the 3 s is hydration and the third-party scripts, below.)

**SEO — 5 errors → 0, 95 warnings → 12.**

| was | now |

| `/about` carried the site-wide default description, so `/` and `/about` shared one | its own sentence |
| every book's description was its subtitle: two novels shared "A Small Town Romance" (20 characters), four had under 40 | `bookDescription`: a long subtitle is already a description and is used; a short one is a tag, so it leads and the book's own opening follows — whole sentences, never longer than a search result shows (`clampText`, 160). All 36 are unique, none is a bare tag |
| category pages: "Browse Romance on Valice Press." (31–45 characters) ×7; an author with no bio: "Books by X on Valice Press." | the editorial copy if there is some, else what is on the shelf: "Romance — 4 books from Valice Press: Weather Permitting, The Sweetest Season and 2 more." |

What the audit checks that is *not* a defect today and stays as a regression test: one canonical per indexable page, on the site's origin and equal to its own path; `og:*` and `twitter:*` complete and the share image answers `image/*`; JSON-LD parses, `@id`s unique, a Book with an author on every book page, a `Person` on every author, **no `Offer` with a zero price** (`price_cents = 0` means not sold here), no `aggregateRating` without reviews; the sitemap lists exactly the indexable pages (no `noindex` page in it, no disallowed URL in it); no public link into `/admin`; every internal link answers; **each ASIN appears on one book's page only**; the 404 is a real 404 and `noindex`.

**Accessibility — axe-core WCAG 2.2 AA + best practice, 28 pages × desktop and phone: 2 findings → 0.** The `<aside>` nested in another landmark on book pages (a note about print editions) and on blog posts (the reading sidebar wrapped a `<nav>` that is a landmark already) are a `role="note"` and a `div`. The card-title heading order (above). Plus what axe cannot see: every control the Tab key reaches in the first thirty stops of four pages shows a focus indicator; the catalogue's sort menu, grid/list toggle and Filters button are **44 px on a phone** (they were 36 and 32); modal focus trapping is in `overlays.pw.ts` already. Alt text: **1199 images across the sitemap — 511 decorative (`alt=""`, by design: the title is next to the cover), 688 described, 129 distinct texts ("Cover of …", a portrait's caption), none that is a file name or a placeholder.** Nothing was hidden from screen readers to pass: the sr-only text added is the full label of a shortened chip.

**Performance — image-heavy site, measured, not guessed.**
- `sizes` told the browser an image was as wide as its container when it filled a fraction of it. The fan of three covers on each category card (each is 38% of the card) and the "Look inside" strip (a page is 150 px wide in a strip as wide as the screen) made a phone fetch **1080 px files for 140 px slots**. Now each says what it draws (`scaleSizes`, `tileSizes`, tested). **`/about` on a phone: 3.6 MB of images → 0.83 MB (page 4.7 → 1.9 MB on the wire); desktop 1.6 → 0.4 MB. A book page's images on a phone 0.93 → 0.71 MB.** Pinned by `e2e/quality.pw.ts` (no image more than 2.3× the pixels the device needs — one rung of the optimiser's width ladder; the bug was 2.6–4.2×).
- **Static pictures had no cache lifetime.** Everything under `/images` is served by Next with `max-age=0`, so a returning visitor re-asked for every cover on every page. `/images/**` is now `max-age=86400, stale-while-revalidate=604800` (a replaced cover shows within a day, and nobody waits for it). The optimiser's output was already 4 h.
- **AVIF was measured and not enabled.** At the same quality setting it saves 3–20% on the covers (about 8%: 68 vs 72 kB, 128 vs 149, 156 vs 161, 57 vs 72), but the sources are lossy WebP, so an AVIF copy is a second lossy generation of art whose quality is the product. Not worth a few kilobytes.
- The authors page's LCP was a decorative backdrop that was not preloaded while six portraits that are not LCP were: backdrop `priority`, portraits 6 → 2.
- `/cart`, `/search` and the library read the whole published list from Postgres on every request (`/cart` took 1.1–3.7 s on production); they use `listPublishedBooksCached` now (an hour, shared by every visitor; ISR pages keep the uncached reader so a catalogue load shows as soon as before). Locally `/cart` is 1.16 s on a cold cache and 15 ms after.
- The hidden **legacy `<SiteHeader>`** (a whole header, its search form and a cart client component, sent with every page and displayed on none — every route is `.cinematic-root`) is deleted, with the CSS rule that hid it.
- `validateEventPayload` was exported from `api/events/route.ts`, which Next's route type check refuses under webpack (Turbopack, which ships, does not enforce it): moved to `lib/event-payload.ts`.

**What is left, measured.** Every page ships **563 kB of JavaScript over the wire (1.97 MB raw, 35 files)**, whatever the page: Clerk's two remote bundles (127 kB gz) and the chunk that carries Sentry's client (77 kB gz) are the largest items, loaded for visitors who never sign in. On the throttled phone they cost **300–500 ms of First Contentful Paint and 30–95 ms of Total Blocking Time** (FCP 1.66 → 1.36 s on `/`, 1.94 → 1.47 s on `/about`; TBT 244 → 149 ms on `/about`; measured by blocking those hosts). Removing them for anonymous visitors means mounting `ClerkProvider` only where a session matters (the header's account button, the review form, `/account`) and loading Sentry after the page is interactive — an architecture change with auth consequences, so it is a decision, not a fix made here.
